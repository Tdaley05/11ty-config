Why Ownership Matters

Buying vs. Renting Your Media

When you buy a physical music, movies, or video games cartridge, you own a copy outright. You can lend it to a friend, play it offline forever (my favorite part), and keep it on your shelf regardless (since streaming services constantly add and remove media).

When you buy a digital movie or game, you are usually only purchasing the license to access that file, which means the company can alter or remove it at any time.

The Trap of Digital Licensing

Why Purchased Media Disappears

Digital storefronts rely on temporary contracts.

When those contracts expire, companies regularly delist games, movies, and shows.

This sometimes wiping them from user libraries even after you paid full price.

Beyond complete removal, digital updates can silently edit out original soundtracks, or alter scenes

(I have a movie I bought on YouTube forever ago, and 2 voices changed since I bought it and I hated when I found that out).

Scarcity and Supply

The Push Away From Discs and Cartridges

Major console makers and retailers are actively phasing out physical media to cut manufacturing costs and push consumers toward subscriptions and digital games. Many new gaming consoles ship without disc drives (such as the next playstation), and big-box stores have dramatically shrunk their physical movie aisles. Second hand stores try to combat this lack of physical media on shelves.

As physical runs get smaller, copies become harder to find at retail price, driving up secondhand market costs and making physical ownership feel like a luxury.

Preservation and Action

Protecting Your Media Collection

When media exists only on outside servers, entire eras of film, television, and gaming history are at risk of being lost forever if a platform shuts down. Physical discs, cartridges, etc. ensure cultural works remain playable decades later.

Beginners can protect their collections by backing up their personal media, as well as other ways.
